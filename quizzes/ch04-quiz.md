## Answer Key

### Multiple Choice

| Question | Answer | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | B | Grounded AI is anchored to user-provided sources; it generates and cites only from those sources |
| 2 | D | Gap-finding questions ask the AI to identify what's present in one source but absent from another |
| 3 | C | Searching poorly organized materials is extraneous load — cognitive work that adds nothing to learning |
| 4 | C | Voice recording directly in NotebookLM is not a supported source loading method |
| 5 | C | The Redundancy Principle states that presenting the same information in two simultaneous formats (audio + same-text visual) causes interference, not reinforcement |
| 6 | C | The defining difference is that NotebookLM is bounded by user-provided sources and will not draw on training data |
| 7 | B | The expertise reversal effect describes how scaffolds that help novices can hinder experts by creating unnecessary interference |
| 8 | B | The Modality Principle states that narration + visual (or narration alone) engages the auditory and visual channels efficiently — audio activates the auditory channel without competing with visual text |
| 9 | C | Read-only sharing gives students the chat interface and generated materials without allowing source modification or access to original documents |
| 10 | B | The Coherence Principle predicts that adding non-essential material — even interesting material — consumes working memory, reducing available capacity for core content processing |

### Short Answer — Grading Rubric

**Question 11** (grounded AI): Full credit responses should define grounded AI as bounded by user-provided sources, explain why accuracy matters in educational contexts, and provide a specific example (e.g., avoiding hallucinated citations, preventing invented curriculum standards, verifying claims against an uploaded document).

**Question 12** (extraneous vs. germane load): Full credit responses should correctly identify that the audio overview reduces *extraneous* load (retrieval and organizational logistics) rather than *germane* load (deep conceptual processing), and that the freed working memory capacity can then be directed toward application, discussion, or higher-order tasks. Partial credit if student conflates the two load types.

**Question 13** (Mayer principles): Full credit responses should accurately describe two principles (Coherence, Signaling, Modality, or Redundancy), connect each to a specific action a teacher would take (e.g., removing non-essential content, adding headings and bold text, choosing audio OR text rather than both, removing redundant simultaneous formats).
