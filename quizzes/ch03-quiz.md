## Multiple Choice (10 questions, 1 pt each)

**1.** In Vygotsky's sociocultural theory, the "Zone of Proximal Development" (ZPD) is best described as:

a) The full range of tasks a student can complete without any assistance  
b) The cognitive space between what a learner can do alone and what they can do with guidance  
c) A method for grouping students by academic achievement level  
d) The maximum difficulty level any student can ever reach with support  

**2.** A Gemini "Gem" is best defined as:

a) A premium subscription to Google's AI services  
b) A saved version of Gemini with a custom system prompt that shapes all of its responses  
c) A Google Chrome extension that adds AI features to web browsing  
d) A type of AI-generated image used in educational presentations  

**3.** According to Bandura's Social Cognitive Theory, which source of self-efficacy is considered the MOST powerful?

a) Verbal persuasion from a trusted teacher  
b) Vicarious experiences (watching peers succeed)  
c) Mastery experiences (successfully completing progressively harder tasks)  
d) Physiological states (feeling calm and confident)  

**4.** What is the primary advantage of meta-prompting for teachers?

a) It allows teachers to bypass Gemini's content filters  
b) It lets teachers describe goals in plain language and have the AI generate an optimized system prompt  
c) It automatically shares Gems across an entire school district  
d) It enables Gemini to access the internet and retrieve real-time information  

**5.** Which of the following is NOT one of the five elements of a great system prompt?

a) Role  
b) Context  
c) Constraints  
d) Subscription tier  

**6.** A teacher builds a "Patient Tutor" Gem but finds it frequently gives students direct answers instead of asking guiding questions. What is the most likely cause of this problem?

a) The Gem was built on an outdated version of Gemini  
b) The system prompt lacks sufficient constraints specifying what the Gem should NOT do  
c) The teacher shared the Gem link incorrectly  
d) The student's questions are too advanced for the Gem to handle  

**7.** Vygotsky's concept of the "More Knowledgeable Other" (MKO) in the AI era is best represented by:

a) A student who has already passed the current unit  
b) An AI Gem designed to scaffold learning within a student's ZPD  
c) A standardized test used to assess prior knowledge  
d) An AI tool that provides direct answers to all student questions  

**8.** The "Differentiation Engine" Gem is designed to:

a) Assess student performance and assign grades automatically  
b) Take one lesson and produce three simultaneous adaptations for ELL, IEP, and gifted students  
c) Create entirely new lessons based on state standards  
d) Monitor student engagement during online classes  

**9.** According to the chapter, which pitfall is MOST likely to undermine a student-facing tutor Gem's pedagogical purpose?

a) Writing a system prompt that is too long  
b) Failing to test the constraint that prevents the Gem from giving answers directly  
c) Not including enough example exchanges in the system prompt  
d) Using too casual a tone in the role description  

**10.** What does Vygotsky's ZPD suggest about the *ideal* instructional challenge level for a student?

a) Tasks should be well within the student's current ability to build confidence  
b) Tasks should be impossibly difficult to build resilience  
c) Tasks should be slightly beyond independent capability but reachable with support  
d) All students should receive identical tasks regardless of readiness

**11.** Explain how a well-designed Gemini Gem can address all four of Bandura's sources of self-efficacy. For each source, give a specific example of how a Patient Tutor Gem's design choices would activate it. Use evidence from the chapter.

**12.** A colleague says: "I tried Gemini and the responses are too generic. AI isn't actually useful for teaching." Based on what you learned in Chapter 3, write a 100–150 word response that diagnoses what's likely going wrong and recommends a specific, actionable fix.

**13.** Describe the meta-prompting process in your own words. Then explain why the chapter calls it the "10x skill" — what does a teacher already have that meta-prompting allows them to leverage? Why is this particularly valuable for busy classroom teachers?

## Answer Key

### Multiple Choice

| Q | Answer | Rationale |
|---|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **B** | ZPD is the productive middle space between independent capability and guided capability (Vygotsky, 1978) |
| 2 | **B** | A Gem is a saved Gemini configuration with a custom system prompt |
| 3 | **C** | Mastery experiences — direct success at progressively harder tasks — are Bandura's strongest efficacy builder |
| 4 | **B** | Meta-prompting lets teachers describe goals in plain language and receive an AI-generated system prompt |
| 5 | **D** | The five elements are Role, Context, Constraints, Style, and Examples. Subscription tier is not one. |
| 6 | **B** | Missing constraints are the most common cause of Gem drift away from intended behavior |
| 7 | **B** | An AI Gem scaffolding within the ZPD functions as an MKO (though not a replacement for human MKOs) |
| 8 | **B** | The Differentiation Engine produces ELL, IEP, and Gifted adaptations simultaneously from one lesson input |
| 9 | **B** | A Gem that capitulates and gives direct answers defeats the purpose of the scaffolded tutoring design |
| 10 | **C** | The ZPD targets the productive edge — challenging enough to require support, not so hard as to be inaccessible |
