## Part A: Multiple Choice (5 pts — 0.5 pt each)

**1.** Benjamin Bloom's "2 Sigma Problem" (1984) refers to:

a) The finding that students who receive one-on-one tutoring outperform conventional classroom students by two standard deviations  
b) The difficulty of implementing two different learning theories simultaneously  
c) A statistical error in early educational research  
d) The gap between K–12 education and higher education outcomes

---

**2.** Robert Bjork's concept of "Desirable Difficulties" describes learning conditions that:

a) Are enjoyable and motivating for students  
b) Are challenging in the short term but produce deeper, more durable learning  
c) Should be avoided to reduce student anxiety  
d) Are appropriate only for advanced learners

---

**3.** Which of the following best describes Piaget's concept of "accommodation"?

a) Fitting new information into an existing mental model without changing the model  
b) The process of memorizing new facts through repetition  
c) Restructuring an existing mental schema to incorporate new information that doesn't fit  
d) Adapting classroom environment to support diverse learners

---

**4.** Manu Kapur's research on "Productive Failure" found that:

a) Students learn best when they receive instruction before attempting problems  
b) Students who attempt problems before receiving instruction, and fail, subsequently learn more deeply than those who receive instruction first  
c) Failure should be avoided because it creates negative associations with learning  
d) AI tutors eliminate the need for productive failure by providing scaffolded support

---

**5.** The human prefrontal cortex, which governs impulse control, risk assessment, and long-term planning, is not fully developed until approximately:

a) Age 16  
b) Age 18  
c) Age 21  
d) Age 25

---

**6.** Carol Dweck's research distinguishes between a "fixed mindset" and a "growth mindset." A student with a **fixed mindset** typically:

a) Seeks challenges as opportunities for growth  
b) Treats failure as useful information about what to practice next  
c) Avoids challenging tasks to protect their perception of their own intelligence  
d) Believes that effort is the primary driver of achievement

---

**7.** B.F. Skinner's operant conditioning theory holds that:

a) Learning is primarily a social process mediated by culture  
b) All behavior is shaped by its consequences — reinforced behaviors are repeated, punished behaviors are reduced  
c) Cognitive development follows a fixed sequence of universal stages  
d) Intelligence is determined primarily by genetic inheritance

---

**8.** According to the chapter, in Bloom's Revised Taxonomy, which cognitive tiers can AI handle most competently?

a) Analysis, Evaluation, and Creation  
b) Remember, Understand, and Apply  
c) Synthesis, Evaluation, and Application  
d) Knowledge, Comprehension, and Analysis

---

**9.** "Hebb's Rule" — "neurons that fire together, wire together" — describes:

a) The process by which the brain physically strengthens neural connections through repeated activation  
b) A pedagogical principle for cooperative learning  
c) The mechanism by which AI language models generate responses  
d) A classroom management strategy developed in the 1970s

---

**10.** Which of the following is described in the chapter as a primary risk of students using AI as a bypass rather than as a scaffold?

a) Students become overly reliant on internet access  
b) Students produce work that appears high-quality but fail to build the underlying cognitive schemas, metacognitive skills, and tolerance for difficulty that constitute genuine learning  
c) AI tools increase the gap between high-achieving and struggling students  
d) Students lose interest in reading traditional texts

## Part B: Short Answer (3 pts — 1 pt each)

**Respond to each question in 3–5 complete sentences. Use specific concepts from Chapter 1.**

---

**Short Answer 1:**

Explain Bjork's concept of "Desirable Difficulties" in your own words. Then give one concrete example of how a teacher could design an AI-integrated activity that *preserves* desirable difficulties rather than eliminating them.

---

**Short Answer 2:**

Piaget identified four stages of cognitive development: Sensorimotor, Preoperational, Concrete Operational, and Formal Operational. Choose ONE of these stages and explain: (a) what cognitive architecture characterizes it, and (b) what unique risk or opportunity AI use presents for learners at this stage.

---

**Short Answer 3:**

The chapter argues that "when everyone has the same tools, mindset becomes the differentiator." Explain what this means using Dweck's fixed vs. growth mindset framework. How might the same AI tool either reinforce a fixed mindset or amplify a growth mindset depending on how it is framed and used by the teacher?
