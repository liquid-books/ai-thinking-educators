# Addendum A: Course Map

## About CAI1001C — AI Thinking for Educators

**CAI1001C: AI Thinking for Educators** is a professional development course offered at Miami Dade College, designed to equip K–12 teachers with the AI literacy, pedagogical adaptability, and practical tool fluency they need to thrive in an AI-integrated educational landscape. The course is structured around eight chapters — each one pairing conceptual depth with hands-on application — and is anchored in the understanding that technology adoption without pedagogical intention does more harm than good.

This addendum provides a transparent map of the course: how each chapter aligns to program competencies, what learning theories ground the work, which hands-on labs develop practical skills, and how the course's eight measurable learning outcomes connect to the full arc of the book.

---

## CAI1001C Course Competencies

The following seven competencies define what a successful CAI1001C graduate can do. Each chapter targets one or more of these competencies, building toward a comprehensive professional AI skillset.

```{list-table} CAI1001C Competency Framework
:header-rows: 1
:widths: 8 20 72

* - **No.**
  - **Domain**
  - **Competency Statement**
* - C1
  - AI Literacy
  - Demonstrate foundational understanding of how large language models and AI systems work, including their capabilities, limitations, and the mechanisms behind AI-generated output.
* - C2
  - Pedagogical Adaptation
  - Redesign learning experiences and instructional strategies to account for AI's impact on lower-order thinking skills, repositioning teaching toward higher-order cognition, creativity, and human connection.
* - C3
  - Tool Fluency
  - Apply Google AI tools — including Gemini, NotebookLM, AI Studio, and Google Antigravity — purposefully and proficiently in professional education contexts.
* - C4
  - Ethical Practice
  - Articulate and apply ethical frameworks for AI use in K–12 settings, including issues of equity, academic integrity, data privacy, and age-appropriate deployment.
* - C5
  - Assessment Design
  - Design authentic assessments that remain valid and meaningful in an AI-enabled environment, emphasizing process, reasoning, and real-world performance over rote output.
* - C6
  - Learner Support
  - Use AI tools to support differentiated instruction, including students with IEP/504 accommodations, English Language Learners (ELL), and learners who benefit from personalized pacing and scaffolding.
* - C7
  - Professional Growth
  - Develop a personal AI professional development roadmap aligned with evolving best practices, institutional expectations, and the long-term trajectory of AI in education.
```

## Course-Level Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of CAI1001C, students will have demonstrated all eight learning outcomes listed below. Each outcome corresponds to one chapter of the course and is written as a measurable, observable performance standard.

:::{admonition} Learning Outcome 1 — Chapter 1: The Classroom Has Already Changed
:class: tip
By the end of this course, students will be able to **identify the specific cognitive tasks in their instructional practice that AI tools have displaced or compromised**, and articulate why a Bloom's-informed pedagogical response is necessary.
:::

:::{admonition} Learning Outcome 2 — Chapter 2: Inside the Machine
:class: tip
By the end of this course, students will be able to **explain in plain language how large language models generate responses**, including the concepts of token prediction, training data, and hallucination, and apply this understanding to critically evaluate AI-generated content.
:::

:::{admonition} Learning Outcome 3 — Chapter 3: Gemini and the Art of the Gem
:class: tip
By the end of this course, students will be able to **construct and deploy custom Gemini Gems** configured for specific instructional contexts, student populations, or professional workflows, demonstrating purposeful tool configuration rather than passive use.
:::

:::{admonition} Learning Outcome 4 — Chapter 4: NotebookLM
:class: tip
By the end of this course, students will be able to **design student-facing learning resources using NotebookLM** — including curated source notebooks, interactive Q&A guides, and Audio Overview summaries — aligned to course content and differentiation needs.
:::

:::{admonition} Learning Outcome 5 — Chapter 5: AI Studio
:class: tip
By the end of this course, students will be able to **configure a functional AI Studio agent** using system prompts, role constraints, and instructional parameters, and evaluate the agent's output for accuracy, bias, and pedagogical appropriateness.
:::

:::{admonition} Learning Outcome 6 — Chapter 6: Agents with Google Antigravity
:class: tip
By the end of this course, students will be able to **build and document a multi-agent workflow using Google Antigravity** that automates or accelerates a genuine teacher task, and reflect on how AI delegation affects professional judgment and workload.
:::

:::{admonition} Learning Outcome 7 — Chapter 7: Pedagogy First
:class: tip
By the end of this course, students will be able to **redesign at least one existing assessment** to remain valid, equitable, and meaningful in an AI-enabled classroom, using authentic assessment frameworks and ethical reasoning to justify design choices.
:::

:::{admonition} Learning Outcome 8 — Chapter 8: The Long View
:class: tip
By the end of this course, students will be able to **produce a personalized, 12-month AI professional development roadmap** that identifies growth areas, specific tools and resources, and a strategy for contributing to peer learning within their school or district community.
:::

:::{admonition} A Note on Competency Coverage
:class: important
No single chapter carries a course alone. The competency framework is intentionally cumulative — early chapters build conceptual grounding (C1, C4) that later chapters apply through tools (C3) and professional practice (C7). Instructors teaching individual chapters in a workshop or seminar format should cross-reference this table to ensure that prerequisite competency exposure is addressed.
:::
